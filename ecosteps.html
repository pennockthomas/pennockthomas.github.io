How it works

Three steps, the same ones the app walks you through when you open it.

1 / 3

Swap plastic for things that last

Every challenge is one small swap: a steel bottle instead of a plastic one, a bar of soap instead of a bottle of it. Nothing to buy to get started.

2 / 3

Grow your tree

Areas of your life sit on a tree: the kitchen, the bathroom, on the go. Open one and its challenges unlock, branch by branch, as you go.

3 / 3

Count what you already do

Plenty of swaps are habits you already have. Tick them off and your progress shows you started further along than you thought.

The three tabs

The whole app is a bottom bar with three places to be.

1 / 3

Tree

The home screen: a floating tree of bubbles over a forest photo. Drag around, tap a bubble to open an area, unlock the next tier.

2 / 3

QuickSwipe

A stack of cards for fast decisions. Swipe right to mark a swap as done, left to leave it on your to-do list. A minute is enough.

3 / 3

Progress

One place to see the totals: swaps ticked per area, what’s left, and a summary you can choose to share with friends.

Under the hood

What it’s made of, and why.

1 / 3

One codebase, two platforms

Flutter and Dart run the same app on iPhone and in the browser. The data layer talks to a small storage interface, so files on the phone and browser storage are swappable.

2 / 3

Firebase, kept small

Email sign-in and a single Firestore document per person, with rules that let you read and write only your own. Without an account, everything stays on your device.

3 / 3

Built to be trusted

39 tests cover saving, loading, import/export and restore. Writes are atomic, so a crash mid-save can’t corrupt your progress.

What I learned

The parts that took more than a first try.

1 / 3

Make it feel instant

The first frames used to flash white, then black, before the app appeared. A matching native launch screen and waiting for the background photo to decode removed that.

2 / 3

Going to the web is a design job

The app was phone-shaped. Putting it in a browser meant rethinking storage, centring the tree for any screen size and switching off what a tab can’t do, like daily reminders.

3 / 3

What’s next

Syncing progress between phone and browser, a layout that uses wide screens properly, and more challenges from the people using it.
